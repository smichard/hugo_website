I recently finished *Papenburg*, Philipp Oehmke's follow-up to *Schönwald*. I had read *Schönwald* in 2023 and was impressed by its style and its way of telling one family story through several perspectives. Its open ending left me curious about what might come next.

*Papenburg* answers that question. It picks uo where *Schönwald* left off, follows characters whose lives have moved on, and shifts much of the action to New York. I would strongly recommend reading *Schönwald* first. The pleasure of this book lies in seeing earlier questions taken up again and in understanding what the characters have carried with them.

## The Author

Philipp Oehmke is a journalist and author who leads the culture department at *Der Spiegel*. He worked at the *Süddeutsche Zeitung Magazin* before moving to *Der Spiegel* in 2006, and he ran its New York bureau from 2015 to 2020. That experience matters in *Papenburg*. The New York setting feels observed rather than chosen for effect.

## What the Book Is About

*Papenburg* picks up the lives connected to the Schönwald family and brings the Papenburg family into clearer focus. The narrative moves between perspectives and returns to earlier events through flashbacks. This was already a strength of *Schönwald*, and it works well again because each change of view adds information rather than only changing the angle.

New York gives the novel a wider frame. American politics enters the story, as does the recent history of Silicon Valley and the people around it. Some familiarity with that world helps, especially when Oehmke refers to figures and connections that his characters are familiar with. It is not required reading, but it adds context to the book's view of wealth, influence, and personal ambition.

At its center, though, this remains a family novel. The characters have developed since *Schönwald*, and the book lets their old choices continue to have consequences. Oehmke does not explain everything at once. He gives the reader time to understand why people act as they do, including in the small memories from their childhoods.

## My Assessment

I enjoy Oehmke's writing style. He moves quickly between scenes and perspectives without losing the thread, and the changes in time keep the story alive. The novel has several turns that surprised me. At moments, the plot becomes slightly absurd, but it remains entertaining and keeps its interest in the people involved.

The setting is one of the book's strengths. Oehmke lived in New York for several years, and that knowledge is visible in the detail without turning the city into a travel guide. It is a place where the characters can try to change their lives, but it cannot free them from what came before.

The drama around Christmas brings the book to a strong climax, much like the summer party in *Schönwald*. The ending itself comes abruptly and feels a little rushed. That surprised me after more than 400+ pages that take their time with details and childhood stories. As with *Schönwald*, the story ends open.

## Conclusion

I found *Papenburg* richly written and very enjoyable to read over the summer. It continues *Schönwald* with the same confident narrative style while moving its characters into a different setting and a different phase of their lives.

Readers who enjoyed *Schönwald* should read it. For me, the two novels belong together, and *Papenburg* made me hope that Oehmke will return to these characters in a third volume.
